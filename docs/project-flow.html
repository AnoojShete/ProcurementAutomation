The question in one line

When can a procurement document — an invoice, PO or quote — go straight to approval and payment without a person checking it, while keeping wrong documents below a chosen error budget? And how much does checking it against company records (vendor master, purchase orders, ledger, tax and bank registries) help compared with judging the document alone?

Today in our platform

  • A hand-weighted average of field confidences × OCR quality, threshold 0.8 (never calibrated)
  • About ten hard "force review" rules: look-alike vendor, PO mismatch, duplicate, LayoutLMv3 disagreement…
  • No guarantee on how many wrong documents slip through

What research has (2026)

  • Calibrated confidence + statistical error control for straight-through processing
  • But it judges each document on its own, field by field
  • Its own rule checks "add little signal" on invoices

What products do

  • Rossum, SAP Ariba, Coupa match invoices to vendor master data and POs
  • With hand-set thresholds and tolerances
  • No published error rates or calibration

The gap we found

No study we found measures what enterprise evidence — vendor-master match, PO and ledger balance, earlier invoices, GSTIN/IFSC registries — adds to a calibrated, risk-controlled decision to process a document without review, at the level payments actually happen: the whole document. Our pipeline already computes all of this evidence and stores it per stage.

Gaps, with the evidence against them too

Evidence

Against
Products already match master data and POs; document rule checks added little in Jin et al.

Considered and not chosen: a better vendor-risk model (established field, synthetic data), invoice-fraud detection (no labelled data), licence anomalies (no ground truth), LLM agent orchestration (not a contribution by itself), a new extraction model (crowded field).

Research by theme

Selective prediction & error control

  • Geifman & El-Yaniv 2017 — abstain to hold a chosen error rate (risk–coverage).
  • Mozannar & Sontag 2020 — learning when to defer to a human.
  • Guo et al. 2017 — modern networks are poorly calibrated; temperature scaling.
  • Angelopoulos et al. 2025 — Learn-then-Test: thresholds with a finite-sample guarantee.
  • Angelopoulos et al. 2024 — conformal risk control.

Calibrated straight-through processing (closest)

  • Jin et al. 2026 — perception/layout/validation + risk control; 39–72 % fields auto-approved at ≤ 10 % error on DocILE, FATURA, VRDU; document-internal checks only, per field.
  • Rombach & Mehdiyev 2026 (IJDAR) — conformal sets for receipts, 98.3 % coverage at α = 0.02.
  • Roy et al. 2026 — ConfBench: VLM confidence from near-perfect to badly over-confident.
  • Nguyen et al. 2022 — HYCEDIS confidence engine.
  • Xu 2026 — multi-signal calibration (preprint, details not independently verified).

Document understanding & datasets

  • LayoutLM 2020, LayoutLMv3 2022, Donut 2022
  • PP-OCR 2020, Docling 2024 — the engines we use
  • SROIE, CORD, DocILE, FATURA, VRDU — none links invoices to POs or a vendor master
  • Krieger et al. 2023 — extractors struggle on rarely seen suppliers

AI in procurement & supply risk

  • Guida et al. 2023, Spreitzenbarth et al. 2024, Balkan & Akyuz 2025, Lakhal et al. 2025 — AI in procurement is early and focused on procure-to-pay automation
  • Baryannis et al. 2019, Jahin et al. 2026 — supply-risk ML is mature (why risk scoring isn't our contribution)
  • BPI Challenge 2019 — real purchase-order event log, but no documents

Honest limits

Synthetic company records

The generator decides how detectable errors are — so we vary injection and noise rates, run a public-data check, and don't claim real-world proof.

Our extractor isn't a VLM

Absolute numbers won't match Jin et al.; only comparisons between gates on the same documents are claimed.

Fast-moving field

Three of the closest papers appeared Mar–Sep 2026. The search was web + Crossref, not direct IEEE/ACM database queries; it is re-run before submission.
